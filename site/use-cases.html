One shortcut.
Eight different jobs.

Dictation sounds like one feature. In a working day it turns out to be a dozen small ones — and a few of them aren’t the ones you’d expect.

01Working with AI

Better prompts, because you stop economising.

When you type a prompt you cut corners. You write “fix the login bug” because spelling out the whole situation is another two minutes of typing. So the model guesses, gets it wrong, and you spend those two minutes anyway — plus the round trip.

Speaking costs you nothing extra, so you say the whole thing: what’s broken, what you already ruled out, and what you don’t want touched.

Prompt
Typed in a hurry

fix the login bug

becomes
Said out loud

The login form returns a 500 whenever the email address has a plus sign in it. I think it’s the validation regex in the auth middleware — I’ve already checked the database and the record is fine. Don’t touch the schema, just the validation.

02Code review

The pull request description you’d otherwise skip.

Everyone knows a good description makes review faster, and nearly everyone still writes “minor refactor” and hits create. It’s not laziness — it’s that explaining a change in writing takes longer than making it.

Talk through the diff while it’s fresh and reviewers get the reasoning, the risk, and the bit you actually want them to look at.

Pull request
Typed in a hurry

small refactor

becomes
Said out loud

This splits the payment handler in two so the retry logic can be tested on its own — behaviour is unchanged. The one risky part is the timeout default dropping from thirty seconds to ten, so that’s worth a second pair of eyes.

03Email

Answer email about as fast as you read it.

Most replies aren’t hard. They’re short, polite, and there are forty of them. The thinking takes seconds; the typing is what turns the inbox into an afternoon.

Say the reply the way you’d say it to the person, and send what comes out.

Mail — Reply
What you said

hi ravi um thanks for the invoice i’ve passed it to finance and they’ll get it paid this week uh sorry about the delay we had a change of process

becomes
What SunoFlow wrote

Hi Ravi — thanks for the invoice. I’ve passed it to Finance and they’ll get it paid this week. Apologies for the delay, we’ve had a change of process.

04Chat

Reply without leaving what you were doing.

A message arrives while you’re mid-thought. Answering it means unloading everything you were holding in your head, typing four lines, and trying to find your place again.

Say it, send it, carry on. The context switch is the expensive part, not the message.

Slack
What you said

becomes
What SunoFlow wrote

Can you push the deploy to tomorrow? The migration is still running and I don’t want to risk it going out half-done.

05First drafts

Talk your way past the blank page.

You can explain the proposal perfectly well to a colleague over coffee. It’s only when the cursor starts blinking that it becomes hard.

Explain it out loud, get a messy draft in a couple of minutes, then edit. Editing something is a far easier job than starting something.

What SunoFlow wrote

The idea is that we let people try it for a week and ask for the card at the end. I think that converts better than asking up front, but we should measure it.

06Notes and recaps

Write it up while it’s still in your head.

The five minutes after a call is when you remember everything and have the least patience for typing. An hour later you remember the gist and have lost the specifics.

Talk the recap out before the next meeting starts, and the details survive.

Notes
What you said

okay so from the call um they want a decision by friday they’re happy with the pricing but not the timeline and uh priya is going to send the revised scope on wednesday

becomes
What SunoFlow wrote

From the call: they want a decision by Friday. They’re happy with the pricing but not the timeline. Priya will send the revised scope on Wednesday.

07Bugs and tickets

Repro steps nobody has to chase you for.

A good bug report is mostly detail, and detail is exactly what gets dropped when you’re typing it out for the third time that morning.

Describe what happened out loud, in order, the way you’d tell the person sitting next to you.

Linear — New issue
Typed in a hurry

checkout broken on safari

becomes
Said out loud

On Safari 17, adding a second item to the basket clears the first one. It only happens when you’re signed out — signed in it’s fine — and I couldn’t reproduce it in Chrome or Firefox.

08Writing in English

Write English as fluently as you speak it.

Plenty of people speak English confidently all day and still slow right down when they have to write it. Word order, articles, tense — the things that never trip you up out loud are the things that make writing take three times as long.

Say what you mean in your own words and let the grammar and punctuation sort themselves out.
